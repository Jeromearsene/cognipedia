## Definition

Negativity bias is the tendency to give more weight, attention, and memory to negative experiences than to positive experiences of the same intensity.

## Mechanism

For events of equal intensity, our brain processes negative information faster, dwells on it longer, and remembers it better than positive information. This imbalance shows up in memory, attention, decision-making, and even vocabulary: we have more words to describe negative emotions than positive ones.

## Examples

- A single critical review among a pile of glowing ones stays in memory long after the compliments are forgotten.
- In politics, a negative campaign against an opponent often sticks in people's minds more than a positive campaign about one's own record.
- A couple needs several positive interactions to offset the effect of a single argument.

## Did you know?

In 2001, psychologists Roy Baumeister, Ellen Bratslavsky, Catrin Finkenauer, and Kathleen Vohs reviewed dozens of studies on the subject in a paper that became famous for its very title: [*Bad Is Stronger Than Good*](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.5.4.323) (Review of General Psychology, 2001). Their conclusion: across nearly every domain studied — relationships, learning, health, money — negative events have a stronger and more lasting psychological impact than equivalent positive events.
